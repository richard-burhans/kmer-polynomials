# KP-7: addition-side optimizations for the symmetric hash (2026-11-10)

**Verdict: +18% for the symmetric hash with x real (96.2 → 113.9 M k-mers/s), and +18% with x complex
(85.6 → 101.4). Merged tables give most of it. λ-state alone gives nothing. All variants are bit-identical.**

Code: `kp7_roll_sym` with `params.opt` bits (hash/kp7.c, eb4262f). Run: `hash/run_bench.sh 31 5 throughput` on cs10,
single thread, k = 31, 736.5 M k-mers, 5 interleaved rounds, `-O3 -march=native` for kp7 and ntHash alike. Raw
output: `kp7_addside_2026-11-10.raw.txt` (kp7 commit eb4262f, a clean tree). The benchmark refused to report unless the
XOR of every variant's hashes equalled the plain symmetric hash. It printed "identical: yes".

| method (x real unless noted) | median M k-mers/s | 5 rounds | vs original |
|---|---|---|---|
| ntHash canonical | 244.8 | 236.2–249.3 | |
| original `kp7_roll64` symmetric | 96.2 | 93.8–98.2 | 1.00 |
| `kp7_roll_sym`, no options | 101.0 | 99.9–103.3 | 1.05 |
| + λ-state only | 96.6 | 93.5–98.0 | 1.00 |
| + merged tables only | 111.4 | 105.9–114.8 | 1.16 |
| + lazy reduction only | 107.2 | 103.8–109.7 | 1.11 |
| **all three** | **113.9** | 111.4–117.6 | **1.18** |
| original symmetric, x complex | 85.6 | 84.8–87.8 | 1.00 |
| `kp7_roll_sym` x complex, no options | 88.8 | 82.2–93.5 | 1.04 |
| **x complex, λ-state + merged** (lazy does not apply) | **101.4** | 95.0–103.5 | **1.18** |

Reading:

- **The specialized function alone gives +5%.** It hashes only the symmetric value, and its loop has no mode switch.
- **Merged tables give the most (+10% over no options).** One lookup and one addition per strand replace two of each.
- **λ-state alone gives nothing,** and that is expected. Without merged tables it trades the two "+ λ" additions for two
  "+ λ(1 − x^{∓1})" additions. It pays only inside the merged table, where the constant is folded in at setup.
- **Lazy reduction gives +6%.** It removes the conditional subtraction from the rolling additions, at the cost of a
  wider final reduction.
- **All three: 113.9, 0.47× ntHash** (it was 0.39×). The gap that remains is the 128-bit multiplies themselves: 6 per
  k-mer with x real. ntHash uses rotations and XORs.
- **x complex with λ-state + merged (101.4) is about as fast as the original x-real code.** It has the stronger
  collision bound, (k−1)/q² against (k−1)/q.

Recommendation: make `kp7_roll_sym` with all applicable options the default for symmetric hashing. Next candidates:
computing the symmetric value only for k-mers a sketch keeps (KP-9/KP-10), and SIMD across several sequences.
