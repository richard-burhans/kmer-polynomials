# KP-7 hybrid v1: cheap symmetric gate, provable hash only for kept k-mers (2026-11-10)

Code: hash/kp7.c `kp7_gate_seq` and `kp7_sym_sample` (4ec8fe0). gate = mix(fold(F+λ)) + mix(fold(R+λ)), where mix is a
one-multiply 64-bit scrambler: strand-symmetric, with heuristic collisions. The provable (F+λ)(R+λ) is computed only
where gate < threshold, and is bit-identical to kp7_roll64(SYM) at the kept positions (test_kp7).

## Laila hardware counters (Xeon Gold 6330, job 8a4f2e9d4f7344b4)

The load-only baseline was in the same job (6.91e9 cycles, 20.75e9 instructions) and was subtracted, then divided by
736,547,799 k-mers. Raw log: `laila_counters_2026-11-10/kp7_hybrid.log`.

| method | cycles/k-mer | instructions/k-mer | vs ntHash |
|---|---|---|---|
| ntHash canonical | 23.6 | 110 | 1.00 |
| provable symmetric, every k-mer (`kp7_sym_seq` 2 lanes) | 47.8 | 179 | 0.49 |
| gate only, every k-mer | 37.8 | 133 | 0.62 |
| sample 1/16, provable hash for kept | 37.6 | 148 | 0.63 |
| **sample 1/1000, provable hash for kept** | **33.4** | 142 | **0.71** |

The gate-only figure includes handing every hash back through the benchmark's per-k-mer callback. Sample 1/1000 hands
back almost nothing, so it is the better estimate of rolling plus gate: about 33 cycles/k-mer, against ntHash's 24 for
everything. The remaining cost is the 4 modular multiplies per k-mer in the rolling.

| minimizer density ratio w=10/20/50 | 1.0009/0.9996/1.0039 | 1.0005/1.0002/1.0032 | 1.0001/1.0007/1.0006 |
| context-free gaps > 200 (expected 0.1) | 8 | 59 | 87 |
| longest context-free gap | 729 | 713 | 14,310 |

The gate samples as well as ntHash on density and bit uniformity. Every hash has more long threshold-sampling gaps than
the geometric expectation, which is the repeat effect. The gate's single 14,310 gap, at this one seed, is most likely a
long tandem repeat whose few distinct k-mers all fall above the threshold. Settling whether any hash is worse here
needs several seeds and the gaps checked against repeat annotation. That is the same open item as Marçais's deserts.
