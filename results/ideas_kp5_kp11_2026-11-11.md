## KP-5: RC palindromes and inverted repeats as a polynomial identity

With complement = negation, R(u) = −x^{a−1} p_u(1/x) is the forward polynomial of rc(u). So a window is an RC
palindrome iff F = R, and a right arm v is the reverse complement of a left arm u iff F(u) = R(v), with false-positive
probability ≤ (a−1)/q per compared pair (Eisenstein hash, q = 2⁶¹ − 1). `ir` rolls F and R for every a-mer and scans
spacers 0..G; the reference uses 2-bit packed fwd/rc codes in the same loop (64-bit keys for a ≤ 32, 128-bit to 64).

**Correctness:** identical to the packed reference and to brute force on synthetic data with planted repeats (arms 6,
12, 20, 33, 40, 64), and identical to the packed reference on NC_044378.1 for every case run: 80,594 RC-palindromic
12-mers, 7,760 20-mers, 34,736 inverted repeats with 20-base arms and spacers to 100, 23,560 with 40-base arms.

**Speed (local, single run; laila below):** for arms ≤ 32 the packed codes build keys about 1.5–2× faster (0.36–0.41 s
against 0.56–0.74 s) and the scan costs the same. Past 32 bases the hash's 8-byte keys beat 16-byte codes (scan 3.5 s
against 5.3 s at a = 40).

**Laila** (Xeon Gold 6330, job 9f793f18b3112141, `ideas/kp5_kp11/run_laila.sh`; phase timings printed by the program,
load excluded; raw in `ideas_kp5_kp11/`):

| case | count (both methods) | hash keys | pack keys | hash scan | pack scan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RC palindromes, k = 20 | 7,760 | 0.79 s | 0.38 s | 0.07 s | 0.07 s |
| inverted repeats, arm 20, spacer ≤ 100 | 34,736 | 0.64 s | 0.48 s | 6.78 s | 6.81 s |
| inverted repeats, arm 40, spacer ≤ 100 | 23,560 | 0.63 s | 0.58 s (128-bit) | 7.37 s | 8.07 s |

Same counts and checksums for both methods in every case. For a = 20 the packed codes are faster overall; for a = 40
the hash's 8-byte keys make the scan 9% faster than 16-byte codes.

**Verdict:** correct, but for arms that fit in 64 bits it is just a slower way to compare packed codes. It earns its
keep only for long arms (> 32, and any length > 64, where packed codes stop being one or two words): a constant 8-byte
key with a provable false-positive bound. It finds only PERFECT inverted repeats; tools like EMBOSS einverted score
approximate arms, a different problem, so they were not used as a baseline.

## KP-11: generating functions

**The ordinary form does nothing.** With F_S(y) = Σ y^{code(s)}, the product F_U(y^{4^{|v|}})·F_V(y) = Σ y^{code(uv)}, and
code(uv) = code(u)·4^{|v|} + code(v) is injective in (u, v). No two terms share an exponent, so the product is the
Cartesian product U × V written as a polynomial. It aggregates nothing, and no FFT can beat listing the pairs. The same
holds for candidate (i), counting formable (k₁+k₂)-mers: they are exactly the |U|·|V| concatenations, and counting how
many occur in a genome is a hash join that the polynomial does not speed up.

**The group-algebra form does work: mismatch-neighbourhood counts.** View a k-mer as a vector in (Z₄)^k. Hamming distance
is the weight of the difference vector, so N_d(t) = #{occurrences s : H(s, t) ≤ d} = (C ∗ B_d)(t), a convolution over
(Z₄)^k, where C is the k-mer count table and B_d the weight-≤-d indicator. The characters are i^{a·m}: the fourth roots
of unity of the original encoding. B_d's transform depends only on wt(m) (a sum of Krawtchouk polynomials, q = 4), so
N_d = IDFT(DFT(C) · K) for ALL 4^k targets in O(k·4^k), whatever d is. `mismatch` computes it exactly with an NTT mod
998244353 (which has a primitive 4th root); every true count is below the modulus, so the residues are the integers.

**Correctness:** identical to direct ball enumeration over all 4^k targets (k = 6, 7, 9 synthetic; k = 10, 12 on
NC_044378.1, d = 0–3) and to brute force on sampled targets.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Verdict:** a real, exact method for "how many k-mers in this genome are within d mismatches of each possible k-mer",
the table behind mismatch kernels and mismatch-tolerant k-mer counting. It wins whenever the ball is larger than a few
dozen, and its cost does not depend on d. Limits: memory and time are O(4^k), so it fits k ≤ 13 (k = 13 needs 0.5 GB),
and it gives counts for all targets at once rather than a query structure. It is very likely a known technique (it is
the MacWilliams/Krawtchouk structure of the Hamming scheme; mismatch-kernel literature uses related transforms), so it
should be cited as an application, not claimed as new, until a literature check says otherwise. It is not the
"generating function" KP-11 imagined: concatenation never aggregates; the group (Z₄)^k does.
